**A note on fortune tellers.** The fortune teller entry above covers operating a prediction service: concrete claims about what will happen to a buyer, and readings framed as medical, legal or financial advice. It also covers open-ended personal consultations sold as access to a practitioner who will tell the buyer what to do.

It does not cover astrology, tarot, numerology, birth-chart and divination material sold for insight, reflection or entertainment. That work has its own [Discover category](https://gumroad.com/self-improvement/spirituality/mysticism/tarot), and courses, decks, guidebooks, chart-generation tools and pre-written reports are all allowed. A one-off personalized reading is also allowed when it is sold as reflection or entertainment and makes no concrete promise about what will happen; what is not allowed is ongoing access to a practitioner who tells the buyer what to do.

The test is what the product promises, not what tradition it draws on. If it guarantees an outcome or substitutes for a professional the buyer should be seeing instead, it is prohibited. If it offers an interpretation the buyer is free to take or leave, whether they work through it themselves or a practitioner prepares it for them, it is allowed.

**A note on gambling.** The gambling entry above covers wagering itself and the things that drive a wager: picks, predictions, tips or "signals", live odds feeds, sportsbook affiliate and promo-code funnels, lotteries, and any promise by the seller that the buyer will make money. It also covers storefronts that present themselves as a casino, sportsbook or betting brand.

It does not cover material that only informs or records. Educational writing and courses about strategy, discipline, house edge or bankroll math are allowed. So are bet logs, bankroll and session trackers, profit-and-loss spreadsheets, odds and expected-value calculators that work on numbers the buyer supplies, and statistical research datasets. A tracker records bets that have already been placed, which is not forecasting.

The test is what the product does with the wager, not whether it mentions betting. If a product decides the wager for the buyer, promises a profit, or routes them to an operator, it is prohibited. If it teaches or keeps records, it is allowed. Naming a sportsbook to describe compatibility or audience is not by itself promotion.
