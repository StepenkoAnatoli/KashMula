## File formats and size limits

Two practical rules follow. For marketplace main images, download on pure white: Amazon, for example, asks for a main image on RGB 255, 255, 255 and at least 1,000 pixels on the longest side for zoom, as our [Amazon product photography guide](https://camclo3d.com/blog/amazon-product-photography) explains. For banners, ads and new scenes, keep a transparent PNG or WebP so you can place the product anywhere. ## How to test edges on your own products

Every vendor shows a clean demo. Your own products are the test that matters, so run the same small set of photos through two or three tools before you commit to a plan. Include:

- **Hair and fur.** A model shot with loose strands, or a faux-fur or knitted item. Look for a halo of the old background between the strands.
- **Transparent and reflective items.** A glass bottle, a clear case, sunglasses. Check that the see-through parts stay see-through, and have not turned solid or been cut away, and that no reflection of the old room is left inside the glass.
- **Fine fabric.** Lace, mesh, tulle and chiffon. Check that the holes in the lace are cut out and that sheer fabric has not turned opaque.
- **White on white.** A white product on a white backdrop. Check for bitten edges where the two meet.
- **Shadows.** Decide whether you want the contact shadow kept, removed or added back, and check what each tool does with it.
- **A dark background check.** View every cutout at 100% zoom on black as well as on white. Fringes that hide on white show up on dark.

If a cutout is close but not perfect, a refine brush, like the ones in CamClo AI and Canva, fixes it faster than a re-run. If it has to be perfect at scale, such as a glassware catalog, hand masking costs more per image but removes the checking step. And if the problem is a stray object rather than the background, a [magic eraser tool](https://camclo3d.com/blog/magic-eraser-tool-remove-objects-photos) is the better fix. Our guide to [ecommerce product photography](https://camclo3d.com/blog/ecommerce-product-photography) covers how to shoot products so they cut out cleanly in the first place.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best AI background remover for product photos?

For ecommerce teams, CamClo AI: a cutout costs 0.5 credits, downloads as a transparent PNG, a WebP or a JPG on pure white, and the same credits then take the product into new backgrounds, on-model photos and ads. For the lowest cost per cutout at volume, look at Pixelcut, Photoroom and Claid. For glass, fine hair and lace that must be perfect, a hand-editing service such as Path is the safest choice.

### Is there a free AI background remover?

Yes. Adobe Firefly removes backgrounds for free with an Adobe account and downloads a PNG. Pixelcut's web remover gives free HD downloads without a watermark, within its free plan's limits. Photoroom's free plan allows 100 exports a month, and Removal.ai and remove.bg give free previews at 0.25 megapixels. CamClo AI has a 7-day free trial with 20 credits.

### What is happening to remove.bg?

remove.bg says its standalone website will no longer be available from 1 December 2026 at 9:00am CET. Background removal moves into Canva, which remove.bg says uses the same technology, and the self-service API moves to Leonardo.ai. Unused pay-as-you-go credits expire on 1 December 2026, and enterprise API contracts continue as normal.

### How much does background removal cost per image?

About 1 to 3 cents per cutout on Pixelcut, Photoroom and Claid plans used in full, $0.02 on Photoroom's Basic API, $0.05 on Pixelcut's API, and about 4 to 6 cents on Removal.ai's monthly plans. On CamClo AI a cutout is 0.5 credits, about 16 cents on the $49 Creator plan, and the same credits also cover new backgrounds and on-model photos. Hand editing at Path starts at $0.39 an image, or $1.19 for hair and fur masking.

### Can I remove backgrounds from hundreds of product photos at once?

Yes, with limits that differ by tool. CamClo AI's AI Assistant runs one workflow over up to 500 product images and downloads the results as one ZIP. Canva processes up to 50 images in a folder at a time, Picsart's Pro plan bulk edits up to 50 at once, and Pixelcut says its bulk remover handles up to 10,000 images in one batch, with 1,000 batch exports a month on Pro. Claid lists batch processing on its Pro and Business plans. For automated pipelines, Photoroom, Pixelcut and Claid all sell APIs.

### Should I download a cutout as PNG, JPG or WebP?

Use PNG or WebP when you need a transparent background, for example to place the product on a new scene or a colored banner. Use JPG on pure white for marketplace main images, since JPG cannot store transparency. Keep the full-size original too, so you can cut it out again if an edge needs fixing.
