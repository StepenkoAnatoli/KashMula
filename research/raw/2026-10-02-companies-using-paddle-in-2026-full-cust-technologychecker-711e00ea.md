## [What Is PADDLE.NET on Your Bank Statement?](https://technologychecker.io/technology/paddle\#what-is-paddle-net)

A **PADDLE.NET\* charge on your card is legitimate** — it is a payment to a software company that uses Paddle as its payment processor, so Paddle appears on the statement instead of the app you actually bought. It is not fraud, and you do not have an account with Paddle itself.

- **Why Paddle's name and not the app's:** Paddle is the _merchant of record_, meaning it is the legal seller for the software vendor. The vendor's name normally follows the asterisk — `PADDLE.NET*GOODNOTES`, `PADDLE.NET*CRASHPLAN`, `PADDLE.NET*MACPAW.COM`.
- **Which apps use it:** we detect **4,052 domains** currently running Paddle, including GoodNotes, n8n, MacPaw, Any.Run, Investing.com, TinyURL, Time Doctor and Anytype. **31.08%** of them are software companies, which is why the descriptor shows up most often after an app or Mac utility purchase.
- **If you don't recognise the vendor:** the charge descriptor names it. Search the text after the asterisk — that is the company you bought from.
- **To cancel or get a refund:** contact the vendor named in the descriptor, not Paddle and not your bank. Paddle bills on the vendor's behalf, so the vendor authorises the refund and Paddle processes it. Filing a chargeback first usually just suspends your software licence.

**Is PADDLE.NET legit?** Yes. Paddle is a UK-headquartered payments company that has been selling software on behalf of vendors since 2012, and it ranks **#23** in our Payment Processing category. An unrecognised `PADDLE.NET*` line is almost always a subscription renewal for software bought months earlier, or a purchase made by someone else on a shared card — not a compromised account.

## [What Is Paddle and What Does It Do?](https://technologychecker.io/technology/paddle\#what-is-paddle)

Paddle is a **payment and billing platform for software companies** that operates as merchant of record — it sells the software on the vendor's behalf and absorbs sales tax, VAT, fraud and chargeback handling. It is built for SaaS and digital products, not physical goods.

- **What merchant of record means in practice:** Paddle is the legal seller, so it registers and remits VAT and sales tax in each buyer jurisdiction. That is the main reason small software companies pick it over assembling tax compliance themselves.
- **Scale and mix:** **4,052 domains** run Paddle in our detection data, concentrated in software development (31.08%), technology and internet (15.10%) and IT consulting (11.28%). **74.31%** have 1–10 employees.
- **How it compares to Stripe:** they are layered rather than alternatives, and we publish no switching figures between them — of the domains that have ever carried both, **94.3% still carry both today**. Stripe is a payment gateway; Paddle is a gateway plus the tax and compliance layer on top, so most Paddle merchants have a processor underneath.

## [Paddle Usage Statistics](https://technologychecker.io/technology/paddle\#usage-statistics)

We publish the level rather than a growth rate: the corpus expanded 17.9% between June and July as our crawler resolved more subdomains, so the step is coverage. What the window measures honestly is stability — only **273 domains have dropped Paddle since we began tracking it in March 2026**, about 6% of the active base. That is the lowest churn ratio in this refresh, and two things drive it. The product genuinely is sticky — a merchant of record handles tax registration and compliance, so leaving is a legal exercise rather than a config change. But the measurement helps: Paddle is matched partly through a `paddle-verification` DNS TXT record, which nobody removes after switching, so 6% is a floor on real churn rather than a reading of it.

## [Expert Analysis: Paddle Growth Trends & Key Signals for Sales Teams in 2026](https://technologychecker.io/technology/paddle\#expert-signals)

![Mehmet Suleyman](https://technologychecker.io/images/team/mehmet-suleyman-ceo.jpg)

[Mehmet Suleyman](https://technologychecker.io/authors/mehmet-suleyman) CEO & Co-founder, TechnologyChecker

I've spent the last decade building web crawling and technographic data systems, and Paddle is one of the more interesting cases in our dataset — the most single-mindedly focused customer base we measure. This analysis draws on **2,355 LinkedIn-enriched companies** matched to **4,052 active detections** as of our August 2026 crawl, G2 review sentiment, and vendor-published case studies. Context worth having: Paddle raised $25M from CIBC Innovation Banking in 2025 and processes $36B in ARR across 4,000+ B2B software companies (per SaaStr). They also paid $5M to settle FTC allegations in June 2025 relating to payments processed for deceptive tech-support schemes — not representative of the typical Paddle customer, but worth knowing.

### 1\. One business model, not one industry

**Software Development alone is 31.08%** of Paddle's enriched base, with Technology/Internet at 15.1% and IT Services at 11.28% — over **57%** in three tech verticals. That is roughly four times Stripe's software share and the most concentrated industry distribution of any payment platform we track.

The named customers show what actually unites them, and it is not industry. **MacPaw** (my.macpaw.com), **n8n**, **GoodNotes** (marketplace.goodnotes.com), **Time Doctor**, **Any.Run**, **Anytype**, **TinyURL** and **Investing.com** are all selling a digital product across borders. **Dürr Dental**, a German dental equipment manufacturer, is the exception that proves it — they sell software alongside the hardware.

> "Paddle's customer base isn't defined by size, country or sector. It's defined by a single problem: you want to sell software to the world and you do not want to register for VAT in forty jurisdictions." - **Mehmet Suleyman**, CEO at TechnologyChecker.io

**Sales Signal:** Target by business model, not vertical. An independent software vendor with international customers is a Paddle prospect regardless of whether it sells to dentists or developers.

### 2\. What the trend line shows

Our current-crawl series runs **3,209 (June) → 3,769 (July) → 4,052 (August 2026)**. We publish the level and not a growth rate: the corpus expanded 17.9% between June and July as our crawler resolved more subdomains, faster than Paddle's own line moved. The figure that carries real information is churn — **273 domains dropped since we began tracking in March 2026**, about **6%** of the base. That is the lowest churn ratio in our current refresh cycle, and the product explains it: a merchant of record holds the tax registrations and the customer relationship, so leaving is a legal exercise rather than a config change.

Paddle's self-reported 4,000+ customers sits close to our 4,052 detections, but the two are not the same set — server-side and mobile SDK integrations leave no browser trace, and some detections are marketing sites rather than customers.

**Sales Signal:** Even read as a floor, 6% churn means this base is sticky. Displacement campaigns will fail; integration, analytics and growth tooling sold alongside will not.

### 3\. Customer profile

**74.31% have 1-10 employees** and another 20.27% sit at 11-50, so **94.6% of the base is under 50 people**. Exactly **two organisations** in the enriched sample have 10,001+ employees. On age, **48.6%** were founded in the 2020s and **42.02%** in the 2010s — over 90% of Paddle's customers are less than fifteen years old, the youngest base in this category by a wide margin.

**Sales Signal:** This is a self-serve, founder-led buying motion. There is no procurement cycle to navigate and no enterprise segment to build for.

### 4\. Geography, and why Bangladesh is not noise

The United States leads at **22.76%** — the lowest US share of any payment platform in this refresh — with the UK at 8.87%, **Germany at 6.03%**, India at 4.88%, and the Netherlands and **Bangladesh tied at 2.51%**. That last figure looks like an anomaly and is not. Paddle's value is highest exactly where solving cross-border tax compliance alone is hardest, so a small software vendor in Dhaka selling to the US and EU has more to gain from a merchant of record than a US vendor selling domestically.

**Sales Signal:** The non-US majority is the story. Localisation, currency and tax-content marketing will outperform US-centric messaging into this base.

### 5\. Why there is no migration section on this page

Paddle is a merchant of record that sits _in front of_ a processor rather than competing with one. Of the domains that have ever carried both Paddle and [Stripe](https://technologychecker.io/technology/stripe), **94.3% still carry both**. They are layered by design, so a switching table would be reporting an architecture as though it were a decision.

**Sales Signal:** Never pitch Paddle against Stripe or vice versa. The honest framing is what the merchant offloads — tax registration, invoicing, chargeback liability — not which logo processes the card.

### 6\. Technology ecosystem

The standout is **Profitwell at 26.82%**: more than a quarter of Paddle domains run it, and Paddle acquired ProfitWell in 2022, so this is the same vendor appearing twice in one stack. Beyond that, **Google Analytics 60.57%** and **Cloudflare Browser Insights 24.61%**, with **Plausible at 5.57%** — far above its corpus-wide rate and a clear marker of a privacy-conscious, developer-led base. Frameworks are diverse rather than dominated ( **Vue.js 10.5%**, React 9.16%, Alpine.js 4.18%), and **WordPress is only 20.41%**, low for our corpus, because software vendors tend to build their own marketing sites.

**Sales Signal:** The Plausible and Cloudflare skew says this base opts out of default tooling. Products sold on privacy, self-hosting or data ownership have an unusually receptive audience here.

### 7\. G2 review signals

Reviewers consistently praise the tax and compliance handling — the reason to buy a merchant of record at all — and consistently raise pricing, since Paddle's take rate sits above a bare processor's. Both are structural rather than fixable: you pay more precisely because Paddle assumes the liability.

**Sales Signal:** The pricing objection is where competitors attack and where Paddle's 6% churn shows they mostly fail. Merchants do the maths once and stay.

### Key takeaways

1. **The most concentrated base we measure:** 31.08% Software Development, 57%+ across three tech verticals.
2. **Defined by business model, not vertical:** independent software vendors selling internationally.
3. **Stickiest product in the category:** 6% churn since March 2026 against 12-24% for everything else here — and that 6% is a floor, since the DNS marker survives a switch.
4. **Youngest and smallest base:** 94.6% under 50 employees, 90%+ founded since 2010, only two enterprises.
5. **Least US-centric:** 22.76% United States, with Germany, India and Bangladesh all materially represented.

Is Paddle payment safe?

Yes. Paddle is PCI DSS Level 1 compliant and operates as a merchant of record, meaning Paddle handles all payment data directly. Sellers never touch card numbers. Paddle also manages fraud prevention, chargeback handling, and buyer protection across 200+ countries. Our data tracks 4,052 active domains using Paddle, including n8n, GoodNotes, MacPaw, Investing.com, and Time Doctor.

What is the difference between Stripe and Paddle?

Stripe is a payment processor; Paddle is a merchant of record that sits in front of one. Paddle becomes the legal seller, so it handles VAT and sales-tax registration, invoicing, and chargeback liability — which is why its take rate is higher. In our data they are layered rather than alternatives: of the domains that have ever carried both, 94.3% still carry both today. Stripe appears on 215,894 domains against Paddle's 4,052, but they are not competing for the same slot in a stack.

Is Paddle cheaper than Stripe?

Paddle charges 5% + 50c per transaction, higher than Stripe's 2.9% + 30c. But Paddle's fee includes tax compliance, fraud prevention, and chargeback management that Stripe charges extra for. For SaaS companies selling internationally, Paddle's all-in price often comes out lower than Stripe plus third-party tax tools like Avalara or TaxJar.

How much does Paddle charge as merchant of record?

Paddle's standard rate is 5% + 50c per transaction for its complete merchant-of-record service. This covers payment processing, global tax compliance, fraud prevention, and chargeback handling. Volume-based discounts are available. For comparison, Chargebee charges separately for billing ($249+/month) and you still need a payment processor on top.

What is Paddle SaaS billing?

Paddle is a billing and payments platform built specifically for SaaS and digital product companies. It acts as the merchant of record, handling checkout, subscriptions, tax compliance, and payouts in 200+ countries. Founded in London in 2012, Paddle reported $90.9M in 2024 revenue (per GetLatka) and has raised $293.3M in total funding.

Is Paddle better than Lemon Squeezy?

Depends on your scale. Lemon Squeezy offers similar merchant-of-record features at a lower starting price, and Paddle is the larger of the two in our data at 4,052 active domains. We do not publish switching figures between them: the overlaps are small and persistent, so any table would be reporting detection noise rather than merchants changing provider. The practical difference is that Paddle Billing targets larger catalogues and more complex pricing, while Lemon Squeezy optimises for getting a single product selling quickly.

What payment methods does Paddle accept?

Paddle supports credit and debit cards (Visa, Mastercard, Amex, Discover), PayPal, Apple Pay, Google Pay, wire transfers, and localized methods like iDEAL and Bancontact. The available methods vary by buyer country. G2 reviews note that limited payment method options is among the top user criticisms, with 17 mentions on that topic.

How does Paddle handle taxes?

Paddle is a merchant of record, so it is legally the seller of your product. That means Paddle calculates, collects, and remits VAT, sales tax, and GST in every country where your customers buy, and you do not need to register for tax IDs in individual jurisdictions. That is the whole value proposition, and our data shows who buys it: only 22.76% of Paddle domains are US companies, the lowest US share of any payment platform we track, because cross-border tax compliance is hardest for vendors selling out of smaller markets.

Can Paddle be used for physical products?

No. Paddle is designed for digital products, SaaS, and software subscriptions only. Our industry data confirms this: 51.5% of Paddle's customers are in Software Development, Technology, or IT Services. If you sell physical goods, Stripe, PayPal, or Shopify Payments are better fits.

What is Paddle.net on my bank statement?

Paddle.net appears on bank statements because Paddle is the merchant of record for purchases made through its platform. When you buy software or a subscription from a company using Paddle, the charge shows as Paddle.net (or Paddle.com) rather than the software company's name. This is normal and expected behavior.

How often does Paddle payout?

Paddle pays out on a rolling schedule, typically every 1-2 business days once your account meets the minimum payout threshold. Payouts are sent via wire transfer or PayPal. Paddle handles currency conversion, so you receive funds in your preferred currency regardless of what your customers paid in.

Is Paddle or FastSpring better for SaaS?

Both are merchant-of-record platforms for digital products, and Paddle has the larger detected presence at 4,052 active domains. We do not publish switching numbers between them — the overlapping domain counts are too small to support a claim in either direction. In practice Paddle Billing is the newer and more developer-oriented product, while FastSpring has been in the market longer and serves more traditional software vendors.
